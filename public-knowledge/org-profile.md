## Name
Karimu International Help Foundation. A registered 501(c)(3), federal tax ID 32-0227956, based in Palm Desert, California. "Karimu" means "generous."

## What it is (one sentence)
An award-winning, almost entirely volunteer-run NGO, founded in 2008 by Don Stoll and Marianne Kent-Stoll, that partners with rural wards in northern Tanzania to eliminate poverty through an integrated, community-led model — and then exits.

## What it does (five pillars)
Karimu's position is that specialising in one area can *alleviate* poverty but not *eliminate* it, so all five are addressed together:

1. **Sanitation & Clean Water** — community-wide systems; all public water points free.
2. **Health** — hospitals and dispensaries, staff trained to WHO protocols, hygiene education, vaccination campaigns.
3. **Education** — school infrastructure, inclusive sanitation for girls, teaching materials.
4. **Income Generation** — entrepreneurship training, new crops and farming practices, managing land as a business.
5. **Financial Services** — community-led savings groups, financial literacy, access to appropriate credit.

## The Karimu Methodology — Seed / Seedling / Tree
| Phase | What happens | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Seed** — Selection & Assessment | Identify candidate wards with government and partner input, door-to-door survey, meet all constituent groups, produce a strategic plan with community-chosen representatives | ~1 year |
| **Seedling** — Project Execution | Build, and teach the discipline to maintain. Sanitation and water first. MOU per project. Progress reviewed annually, but pace is set by behavioural change, not deadlines | 3-5 years |
| **Tree** — Gradual Exit | Responsibility transfers to the community; Karimu steps back. No target calendar date for exit | ~1 year after the last project |

The stage a ward is in determines what is appropriate to propose. A Seed ward needs trust-building and assessment; a Tree ward needs exit planning, not new capital.

## Where it works
Babati District, Manyara Region, Tanzania.

- **Ayalagaya Ward (2008-2026)** — 41 km², ~2,500 households, ~12,000 people. $6M over 18 years. Poverty eliminated; only 4.9% of households remain poor, down from about half six years earlier. The community now sustains all programming independently.
- **Arri Ward (2021-2028)** — 98 km², ~3,200 households, ~16,000 people. $2.1M invested. Progress outpacing Ayalagaya at the same point. Campaign underway to raise an additional $4M.
- **Dabil Ward (2025-2030)** — 154 km², ~20,000 residents across five largely isolated villages. Selected in 2024; Karimu's most ambitious project to date. Household survey underway.

## The Social Fund
Up to 10% of funds raised is allocated to respond to urgent requests outside the three focus wards — prioritising wards that met the selection criteria but were not chosen (Hidet, Masakta, Masqaroda), and government requests backed by an official letter from the Babati District Director or higher.

**Efficiency.** In 2024, 98.6% of donated funds were directed to Tanzania, where 96.3% went directly to projects. In 2025, 99% of funds were transferred to Tanzania; expenses split 95% programs / 5% Tanzanian management and general, with US management and operations at 0.3% and fundraising at 0.7%. 2025 program spending: 24% Sanitation, 17% Health, 15% Education, 25% Income, 5% Financial Services, 5% Social Fund, 3% impact assessment and planning.

**Selected 2025 delivery.** Over 135,000 patient visits; 3,236 babies delivered safely (426 by C-section); 50,638 vaccines administered with over 90% of young children fully vaccinated; 14,129 students given hygiene education by teachers with no Karimu involvement; average water-system maintenance resolution time down 59%, to under the 30 days Karimu requires.

Tanzanian government awards include Best NGO in Babati District (2023) for health, water and education; Best NGO Manyara Region (2021); Best Health Center Manyara Region (2024, Dareda Kati); Best High School Manyara Region (2024, Ayalagaya High School, ranked #22 nationally); Best Dispensary Babati District (2024, Gajal); Best Performing School in the Nation ranked #3 (2022, Ayalagaya Secondary); Cleanest Primary School in the Nation ranked #3 (2021, Dareda Kati Primary); four-time winner of the Uhuru Torch of Freedom.
