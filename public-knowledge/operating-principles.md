These are Karimu's six published Operating Principles, printed in full in each
Annual Report. They are the filter every project, partnership, budget line and
public statement is run through — not aspirational language.

### 1. Ongoing dialogue must include everyone
Poverty is a complex problem. Its solution requires direct dialogue with the entire community, including the most vulnerable. All constituencies must have a voice in the dialogue.

### 2. Decision making for projects is led by the community
Projects are chosen by respected members of the local community, jointly with Karimu's leadership, to benefit the community as a whole. Karimu seeks consensus and shares plans with the whole community to prevent misunderstanding of its intentions and ensure cooperation. **No project is executed without a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)** between Karimu, the project's beneficiaries, and the local government, allocating responsibilities to each.

### 3. All projects are joint investments between Karimu and the community
Local residents and/or the government must contribute **a minimum of five percent** of project cost in labour, materials, or money. All adults take part, instilling the sense of ownership that becomes critical to maintenance.

### 4. No project moves forward without a solid and committed maintenance plan
Karimu rejects the possibility of a community's long-term dependence on it. Local communities or local government must take responsibility for overseeing and maintaining projects after completion.

### 5. Operate the organization with extreme efficiency to maximize donor investment
A handful of Tanzanian employees run the projects with support from scores of volunteers from more than a dozen countries, and the leadership team works without compensation. In 2024 this directed 98.6% of donated funds to Tanzania, where 96.3% went directly to projects.

### 6. Data driven and transparent
While Karimu respects community decisions, it relies on data to assess and improve projects — surveys to determine need, and regularly updated Health, Education and Water project dashboards.

## Published decision heuristics

Statements the COO and President have made in print, in the Annual Reports, that
show how the principles are applied:

- **"No handouts."** Speed is never dictated by how fast Karimu can build, but by how fast a community can transform its behaviour. Ownership and sustainability are described as "the only metrics that matter." (2025 COO letter)
- **Behavioural change sets the pace, not deadlines.** Even with unlimited resources, communities cannot absorb rapid progress. This is why Karimu operates in several wards at once, reallocating resources according to each ward's readiness. (2024 COO letter)
- **Maintenance is taught before responsibility is transferred.** When Arri's water committees, schools and clinics struggled with maintenance, Karimu stayed patient and balanced incentives with accountability rather than taking over — then resumed construction once maintenance held. (2024 COO letter)
- **Exit is a milestone, not a loss.** There is no target calendar date for leaving a ward; Karimu leaves once the community has established the behaviours needed to sustain itself. (2025 Methodology section)
- **Serve outside the model without breaking it.** Communities that ask for help but do not fit the integrated ward model are served through the Social Fund, capped at 10% of funds raised, rather than by diluting the methodology. (2025 Social Fund section)

## Guardrails for the agent (non-negotiable)
- Never draft or send a commitment — a grant reply, a partnership term, a budget approval, a public statement — as final. A human approves before anything goes out. See the `risk-escalation` skill.
- Never claim an efficiency or impact statistic not verifiable against this repository or an Annual Report. Cite the year.
- Default to the most recent Annual Report's numbers, and flag when a figure may be stale.
